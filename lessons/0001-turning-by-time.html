Turning by time, not by frame

How investigateScanAngle grows a little every frame, and why “a little” has to depend on dt.

The picture

Look at the agent from above. When it arrives at the sound, you freeze one line: investigateFacingYaw, the direction it was facing when it got there. That line never moves during the scan. It is the centre of the sweep.

investigateScanAngle is the gap between that frozen line and where the agent is looking right now. It is measured in degrees. At arrival the gap is 0. Scanning just means changing the gap over time: 0 → 10 → 20 → … → 45, then back the other way.

This lesson covers one question: how much should the gap change in one frame?

Speed × time = distance (for angles too)

You already know this from driving. 60 km/h for half an hour is 30 km. Rotation works the same way:

  • INVESTIGATE_SCAN_SPEED is a rate: 45 degrees per second.
  • dt is a duration: the seconds that passed since the last frame (about 0.0167 at 60 fps).
  • Rate × duration gives an amount: the degrees to turn this frame. The units show it: degrees/second × seconds = degrees.
45 °/s × 0.0167 s ≈ 0.75° this frame

Add 0.75° sixty times and you get 45° after one second, which is what the constant promised.

Why not just add a fixed amount per frame?

Frames don't arrive at a steady rate. Debug builds are slower, a busy scene drops frames, and a 144 Hz monitor runs more of them. If you add a fixed “0.75 per frame”, the agent's head turns faster on fast machines and slower on slow ones. Multiplying by dt makes the small steps bigger when frames are rare and smaller when they're frequent, so every machine gets 45° per real second. Nystrom explains this in his “One small step, one giant step” section; your Jolt movement code already does it with gravity * dt.

Which way: the sign

investigateScanDirection is either +1 or -1. Multiplying the step by it doesn't change its size, only its sign. At +1 the gap grows, so the agent turns one way. At −1 the gap shrinks, so it turns back. Next lesson you'll flip that sign at the limits. For now it stays +1.

new gap = old gap + direction × speed × dt
